@zakkster/lite-pick — the capstone

A load balancer you can watch work.

Eight real workers doing real CPU work. lite-pick chooses who gets each request; per worker a supervisor, a health check and a circuit breaker decide who is up; a supervisor heals what you break. Every part is a published @zakkster/lite-* package, zero-allocation on the request path, and gated in CI like a library.

What happens to one request

  1. arrive Open-loop traffic: Poisson arrivals at the offered rate, each with a Zipf-distributed key. Arrivals never wait for completions, so overload shows honestly.
  2. choose lite-pick picks a worker with the live strategy — one of ten, switchable under load — reading only who is eligible, who is busy and how fast each has been.
  3. run lite-worker-pool posts a ~1 ms job to that one worker: two slots and a capped queue each, results in order, one dead worker never takes the others down.
  4. settle The result feeds the strategy (latency, occupancy), the worker's circuit breaker and the percentiles. A failure fails over once, to a different worker.
  5. heal Twenty times a second the fleet asks each worker's health check and breaker whether it may serve, and writes that — only that — into the balancer. A dead worker is restarted by its supervisor; a crash loop is escalated and kept out until reset.
  6. narrate Every breaker flip, rotation change, restart and failover is an event: the DECISIONS and REROUTES panels are that stream.

Try this in the live system

kKill a worker. Its in-flight requests fail over; its supervisor restarts it; health lets it back in. Nothing is lost.
sSlow one ten times. Under PeakEWMA (7) its share collapses; under LeastConn (4) or RoundRobin (1) the tail latency climbs.
fMake one flaky. Five failures in a row open its breaker; after two seconds one probe decides whether it comes back. Failover hides it from every caller.
lCrash-loop one. Five restarts in thirty seconds and the supervisor gives up: escalated, kept out, until r resets it.
8Route by key. ConsistentHash keeps every key on its worker — and melts the one with the hot keys; BoundedLoad (9) spills the overflow.
qShut it down. The orchestrator drains, waits for every in-flight request, retires each worker and reports its exit code.

Every brick earns its place

Each package has one job here, and a test in pickEcosystem/live/test/ that fails if it stops doing it.

lite-pick1.1.0

Chooses the worker. Ten strategies over typed arrays, allocation-free per pick.

G3: one 10x-slow worker, p99 10 ms under PeakEWMA vs 340 ms under RoundRobin.

lite-worker-pool1.1.0

The workers: a job to a chosen worker, per-worker isolation, respawn, control values.

G2: kill a worker under load, no request lost. R1: 1000 respawns, all collected.

lite-worker1.4.0

A Web Worker from a function (a Blob URL), transfer-only messages.

The browser smoke run boots eight of them in headless Chrome.

lite-di-container2.2.1

The composition root, and one child scope per worker.

R2: 200 scope rebuilds, every old scope collected. The graph above is its own.

lite-di-supervisor1.0.0

Restarts a dead or hung worker; escalates a crash loop and keeps it out.

G6: escalated after five restarts, back after a reset.

lite-di-health1.0.0

Per-worker readiness: the READY handshake, no hung job, a healthy supervisor.

G5: a hung worker is out of rotation within 600 ms, then respawned.

lite-statechart1.1.0

Each worker's circuit breaker: closed, open, half-open with one probe.

G4: the breaker opens with no failed request and closes after healing.

lite-di-strategies1.0.0

The live strategy switch: a new balancer built cold, eligibility replayed.

G8: all ten switched under load, in-flight counts conserved.

lite-di-event-bus1.1.0

Every decision as a numeric event, 0 B per emit; the decision stream.

A1: inside the zero-allocation steady state.

lite-di-cron1.0.0

Traffic at 100 Hz, the fleet at 20 Hz, stats at 1 Hz — on an injected clock.

Every gate runs on a virtual clock, deterministically.

lite-di-orchestrator1.0.0

Graceful shutdown: drain, stop supervising, settle in-flight work, retire, exit code.

G7: exit code 0, every in-flight request settled, workers gone.

lite-di-graph1.0.0

Exports the container's real wiring.

S2: the picture above is regenerated and compared in CI.

lite-sketch1.1.2

Latency percentiles (DDSketch, ±1%), end to end and per worker.

D3: a slow worker's p95 reads several times a normal one's.

Gated like a library

This site is deployed only after every job passes:

  • lite-pick itself — unit tests on Node 20/22/24 × three OSes, allocation gates (with V8 inlining on and off), torture, benchmarks, the packed tarball's types.
  • the kernel — ten scenario gates, each with a control that must fail; allocation rate by scavenge count; retention after 1000 respawns; the same gates against lite-pick's working tree.
  • the surfaces — the TUI scripted and compared byte for byte; the import map pinned to package.json both ways; a 20-second run over real threads; this site, built exactly as deployed, driven in headless Chrome through its own keys.
